Rules & Regulations

How the Brisbane Air 50 works

The short version: spot 50 wild bird species anywhere in Greater Brisbane, log each one, and your name climbs the leaderboard. The long version is below.

Rule #1
Safety First

Nothing about the Brisbane Air 50 is worth risking harm to a participant or to the wildlife itself. This is the one rule that overrides every other rule on this page — no species, no leaderboard spot, and no Hall of Fame induction is worth compromising safety.

See a sick, injured, or dead bird — or one behaving strangely (can't fly, disoriented, unusually unafraid of people)? Don't approach it, and don't log it here. With H5 bird flu confirmed in Queensland wild birds in 2026, report it straight away instead: the 24-hour Emergency Animal Disease Watch Hotline on 1800 675 888 for anything that might be bird flu or an unexplained illness or death, or RSPCA Queensland on 1300 264 625 (7am–7pm) / Wildcare Australia on 07 5527 2444 (24 hours) for general injured-wildlife rescue. A report like this doesn't count toward anyone's tally, and that's fine — it's not meant to.

  • Keep a safe, respectful distance. Never approach, corner, chase, feed, or bait a bird (or any other animal) to get a better look or a photo.
  • Stay clear of nesting and breeding sites — disturbing them can cause birds to abandon eggs or chicks.
  • Respect closures, private property, and any area marked off-limits, and follow the guidance of park rangers or land managers on-site.
  • As habitat-specific variants like Land 50 and Water 50 take shape, expect more encounters with wildlife beyond birds — snakes, spiders, marine stingers, and deep or fast water among them. Know the risks of the terrain you're heading into, and turn back if something feels unsafe.
  • If in doubt, don't. A missed sighting costs nothing; a safety incident affects everyone.

Required to Play
Registration & Liability

Because the challenge carries real physical risk — see Rule #1 above — everyone must complete a one-time registration before logging their first sighting. Simply typing a name into the sighting form is no longer enough. Registering asks for your display name, full legal name, date of birth, residential address, phone number, and email, and asks you to confirm — with a clear checkbox, not a buried assumption — that you've read and understood these Rules & Regulations in full, including Safety First.

If you're under 18, a parent or guardian must also tick a consent box during registration confirming they approve of you taking part. Once registered, you take part by typing your exact display name on Log a Sighting each time — the same simple, no-password approach the site has always used, now behind a proper sign-up first.

Assumption of risk: the Brisbane Air 50 takes place outdoors, in natural environments, where hazards exist — uneven terrain, weather, water, and wildlife, with further hazards specific to Land 50 and Water 50 as those variants launch. By registering, you acknowledge these risks and take part at your own risk.

Limitation of liability: to the fullest extent permitted by law, Curiously Georgie and its organisers accept no liability for injury, loss, or damage arising from foreseeable accidents connected with participation. This does not exclude any liability that cannot lawfully be excluded — for example, liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, where the law does not allow that to be excluded.

Removal from the challenge: a participant who causes abuse or harm to another participant, or to wildlife or nature, may be permanently banned — their registration is suspended and they can no longer log sightings under that name, on this site or via its public API.

This wording is a starting point, not legal advice — it should be reviewed by an Australian solicitor before launch, particularly the liability limitation, the collection of participants' personal information (including from minors), and how all of it sits alongside the Civil Liability Act, Australian Consumer Law, and the Privacy Act.

Please Read
Your Privacy

What registering collects: your display name, full legal name, date of birth, residential address, phone number, and email — plus a parent/guardian consent tickbox if you're under 18. This is collected once, at registration, not every time you log a sighting.

What's ever shown publicly: only your display name (a nickname is fine), your species tally, and your most recent sighting date — on the Leaderboard and Hall of Fame. Your legal name, date of birth, address, phone, and email are never published, and aren't retrievable through the site's public API at all — only Georgie, signed in as the project's administrator, can see them.

Why we collect it: your address is so a finisher's coin can be posted to you if you complete all fifty. Your legal name, DOB, phone, and email are kept as a registration record, and — this is the part worth being upfront about — so that a participant who causes abuse or harm to another participant, or to wildlife or nature, can be identified and permanently banned from playing. Registering doesn't put you under any extra scrutiny beyond that; it exists so the small number of bad-faith cases can actually be acted on.

Sighting and registration data is stored with Supabase, a third-party database provider, hosted in Australia. There's no self-service way to edit or delete your own details yet — if you'd like something corrected, removed, or you'd like to know what's held about you, contact contactcuriouslygeorgie@gmail.com and we'll sort it out directly.

The Brisbane Air 50 is a small, independent project — this notice isn't a formal legal document, just our best effort to tell you plainly what happens to what you submit. Given the amount of personal information registration now collects (including, in some cases, information about minors), this notice and the registration flow should be reviewed against the Australian Privacy Act before official launch — see the note above.

The Challenge

Spot and log 50 different wild bird species within Greater Brisbane. There's no entry deadline and no time limit — it's an ongoing, go-at-your-own-pace challenge you can pick up any week of the year.

Who Can Play

Anyone — backyard birders, families out for a walk, seasoned twitchers with a scope. There's no sign-up fee and no equipment requirement, though a pair of binoculars and a field guide certainly help.

The Boundary

"Greater Brisbane" covers the Brisbane City Council area plus the surrounding region — Moreton Bay, Redlands, Logan, Ipswich, and out to the D'Aguilar Range. A sighting anywhere inside that boundary counts.

What Counts

  • Wild, free-living birds only — no aviary, zoo, or clearly domestic birds (backyard chickens, farmed ducks).
  • Each species counts once per participant. Once you've logged a Rainbow Lorikeet, a second Rainbow Lorikeet doesn't add to your tally — though you're welcome to keep a personal log of repeat sightings.
  • Use the common name from the Species Checklist where you can. A species not on that list still counts, as long as you're confident of the identification.

How To Log It

Submit each sighting through Log a Sighting with your name (or a consistent nickname — the leaderboard needs to recognise you across visits), the species, the date, roughly where you saw it, and a clear photo as evidence. From official launch, a photo is required, not optional.

Every submission starts as pending — it won't appear on the Leaderboard or count toward your tally until it's been reviewed and confirmed, either by an automated check (most sightings, within moments) or by Georgie personally. Manual reviews can take up to 48 hours, so a pending sighting isn't a sign anything's wrong — it just hasn't been looked at yet. You can check the status of your own sightings any time on My Sightings.

Verification & Launch Date

The Brisbane Air 50 only counts sightings from its official launch date onward. Every submitted photo is checked against its original date and time stamp (EXIF metadata) — if that timestamp is from before launch, the sighting won't be confirmed, even if the photo is completely genuine.

This exists so nobody can complete the challenge on day one from an old photo collection — the challenge is about what you spot from launch onward, not what you've already got saved. Submissions that fail the authenticity or timestamp check are rejected with a reason and can be resubmitted with a valid photo.

Leaderboard & Hall of Fame

The Leaderboard ranks everyone by accumulated Air Points from unique confirmed species, highest first. You're free to log birds outside the official 50 too — they still add to your overall tally — but only the official 50 earn Air Points or count toward the Hall of Fame. Confirm all 50 official Brisbane Air 50 species and you're eligible for the Hall of Fame — Georgie does one final manual check over your 50 confirmed sightings (no new submission needed, just a last look at the evidence photos already on file) before you go up permanently, even if you keep spotting birds past that point.

Fair Play

The review step keeps the Leaderboard and Hall of Fame trustworthy, but the spirit of the challenge is still honour and encouragement, not policing. Log sightings you're genuinely confident about, and if you're unsure of an identification, it's fine to ask around before logging it. The point of the Brisbane Air 50 is getting more people outside and looking up, not winning at all costs.

Got a question the rules don't cover? These are the guidelines the whole challenge runs on — fair play and good judgement fill in the rest.
